1 How to read this document
The mission has a connected chain of mathematics. A camera and inertial sensors estimate
the probe’s motion; a dynamic response model converts that motion to an atmospheric wind
profile; a statistical wind model propagates the remaining drift to predict landing. Pressure and
temperature provide density, height and stability. A wrong assumption early in this chain can
produce convincing but biased results later.
Each section explains the model, derives its equations, and gives numerical or limiting-case
checks. For a first reading, follow the physical dynamics, then the camera and filter, then
turbulence, retrieval, and prediction. The supporting-engineering section supplies quantities
needed to make the data collection possible. The final sections distinguish completed verification
from the experiments still needed.

1.2 Important corrections to the proposal
The following are results of the mathematical audit; they do not silently replace a flight design
decision.
1. The lagℓ=v2
d/g follows from isotropic vector drag, not from vertical canopy force balance
alone. A measured lateral response coefficient is needed to justify it.
2. The stated shear example gives a horizontal offset of23.8m over100m of descent. A
vertical-path approximation is conditional, rather than accurate to a few percent at every
scale.
3. The warped turbulence coordinate must increase with descent. A positive time derivative is
inconsistent with an upward-increasing definition.
4. A body-axis accelerometer can be blind to swing under an ideal suspension, but gyro angular
acceleration cannot determine arbitrary static tilt or unknown forcing.
5. A temperature lapse does not determine Obukhov length. The bulk Richardson number is
not the gradient stability theorem, and0.25is not a universal turbulence cutoff.

1.3 Verification approach
Analytic derivations are accompanied by checks that use a different route: direct projection against
homography, finite differences against analytic Jacobians, numerical ODE integration against
closed forms, Fourier quadrature against a rational spectrum, and batch Gaussian conditioning
against Kalman/RTS inference. A successful test is evidence for the checked identity, not for every
untested implementation detail.
Lean certificates are not included. The ordinary linear algebra, calculus and probability identities
are stated with their hypotheses and verified by independent calculations. The remaining uncer-
tainty concerns unmeasured physical parameters, field representativeness, algorithmic assumptions
and finite data; a formal certificate for algebra would not resolve those issues.

8.4 A theorem for a fixed landing point
Let P be thesame final landing pointthroughout the descent, andD the observations already
collected. In a fixed linear Gaussian model, condition on one more observationY. Gaussian
conditioning gives

where C = Cov(P,Y|D)and S = Cov(Y|D). The subtracted term is positive semidefinite.
Thus the absolute landing covariance cannot increase as valid data is added, regardless of negative
transverse correlations. With no new information it remains unchanged. This theorem concerns
a fixed random endpoint, fixed model parameters, nested observations, and correct propagation
of all cross covariances. Parameter refitting, EKF linearisation, model changes, and observation-
dependent rejection can invalidate the theorem’s conditions.

9 Numerical verification and synthetic experiments
9.1 Reproducibility and the scope of the simulation
All persistent calculations use Python and NumPy. The reference scripts are insim/maths;
run_verification.py writes results/verification.json, a test report, and one synthetic
profile CSV. The random seed is 20260930. There are 2000 independent synthetic descents per
scenario, each with two independent horizontal axes. The suite currently has 46 passing tests.
The runs used Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.3.5.
The mathematical reference implements only a small model, not onboard flight software. There
is no terrain-image renderer or satellite-map matcher in these simulations. Projection and filter
equations are checked numerically, but T1’s match reliability, first-fix time and actual position
accuracy have not been established. The “TRN” velocity channel below is a synthetic measurement
channelwithassumednoise; itisnotaresultfromimplementedterrain-relativenavigation. Likewise
the prior-only channel is not a complete inertial dead-reckoning implementation.
The synthetic atmosphere uses mean wind 5 m/s in each horizontal axis, fluctuation standard
deviation 1 m/s,L(z) = 0.5 max(z, 1 m), known constant downward speed 10.5 m/s, and initial
horizontal position standard deviation 2 m per axis. The mean and covariance parameters are
known to the matched estimator. Velocity measurements are at 5 Hz, with independent Gaussian
component noise 0.1 m/s for the GPS-labelled channel and 0.6 m/s for the vision-labelled channel.
The initial coupled turbulence/velocity covariance is the stationary solution at 300 m; the initial
position error is independent. Ground regularisation is a scenario assumption.
The drift state is accumulated from the same uncertain initial position, rather than reset to a
perfectly known current position. The endpoint predictor propagates the entire covariance and
has no mast update. Post-flight wind retrieval uses an RTS smoother. This is a useful consistency
test for the stated probabilistic system, while the nonlinear drag and coloured-noise experiments
deliberately test departures from it.

10.2 Completed and strongest evidence
The proposal’s mathematics has been assembled into a coherent reference with a consistent camera
convention, stated physical assumptions, explicit derivations, corrected probability claims, and
worked examples. The exact turbulence realisation, covariance positivity, descent time conversion,
inverse lag, Gaussian conditioning, smoothing, and fixed-endpoint monotonicity have analytic
derivations. The numerical suite verifies independent formulations and limiting cases, with retained
synthetic outputs.
The strongest simulation result is conditional: matched known parameters yield well-calibrated
landing ellipses and sub-0.5 m/s layer errors in the synthetic case. The strongest engineering
caution is also quantified: unmodelled error correlation severely reduces coverage, and uncalibrated
lateral response can produce order-0.5 m/s wind bias despite perfect motion measurements.
The built-in editor’s compilation was attempted and failed before producing a PDF with the
environment message “Unable to find standard directories for platform.” Source brace/environment
checks, unique-label checks and reference checks are separate from compilation. No rendered page
layout, line overflow or package availability has been verified. The saved standalone source is
preserved for the editor or another LaTeX installation; no compiler or plugin was installed.

10.4 What still requires measurements or project decisions
1. Canopy dynamics.Measure terminal speed, lateral response, deployment transient and
pendulum motion. Fit τh or a lateral drag model with confidence intervals. A vertical
descent-rate measurement alone does not select the horizontal model.
2. Camera and inertial calibration.Determine intrinsics, distortion, extrinsics, lever arms,
exposure timing and timestamp uncertainty. Test map matches over representative terrain;
measure failures, first-fix time, bias and temporal covariance.
3. Truth quality.Survey map control points with quantified uncertainty, and log indepen-
dent position/velocity truth. GPS alone does not supply all fifteen navigation error-state
components for a full-state NEES calculation.
4. Atmospheric inference.Measure thermistor response in flight-like airflow, pres-
sure/density uncertainties, mast representativeness and relevant stability flux information
or jointly identifiable MOST profiles. Assess displacement height and heterogeneous fetch
before reporting roughness.
5. Retrieval independence.Quantify the shared attitude, lag and prior errors in image-
versus-GPS wind comparisons. A common bias can cancel in their difference.
6. Calibration across drops.Evaluate prediction ellipses and time intervals on independent
descents, with predeclared metrics and model tuning separate from validation. A single
flight cannot test 95% long-run landing coverage.
7. Stretch certification.Select the exact registration relaxation, bounds and solver toler-
ances before issuing an SDP certificate; rank appearance alone is insufficient. Safe-target
reachability is hypothetical for a passive canopy.
8. Requirements.Confirm the current competition rules, actual radio configuration, mass
convention, load cases, battery and processing budgets before asserting compliance.
10.5 Highest-value next action
Conduct a short, time-synchronised suspension/drop calibration with image logging, IMU, pressure
and independent velocity truth. Use it first to estimate canopy lateral response and sensor-error
correlation, then rerun the same mathematical model with those measured uncertainties. This
directly tests the two assumptions that dominate the demonstrated gap between an internally
consistent simulation and a reliable wind/landing result. Preserve raw logs and calibration
metadata so later navigation and atmosphere analysis can be repeated.
