## Mission basis

The local [secondary mission proposal](trn_secondary_mission_proposal.md) calls for GPS-denied terrain-relative navigation during a roughly 30-second, 300–400 m parachute descent: 5 Hz global-shutter imagery, 100 Hz navigation, independent GNSS truth, and fast air-temperature measurements. The supplied [UK guidelines in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1LubKeC729i9NbmgHwG-wzu4gwhDO1PfJ/view) require 1 Hz temperature/pressure telemetry, three hours of continuous operation, a 66 mm diameter × 115 mm body, 300–350 g total mass, survival of 20 g, and a £400 flight budget. The document is for 2025–26; confirm the 2026–27 rules before freezing the design.

| Constraint | Design consequence |
|---|---|
| Pi Zero 2 W selected by the user | Pi handles camera, image processing and SD logging; the STM32 handles timing, sensors and radio. Its 512 MB RAM makes bounded image/map buffers essential. Achieving 5 Hz TRN remains a benchmark requirement. |
| Primary mission survives vision failure | Separate battery-fed 3.3 V converter powers the STM32, primary sensors and radio. The STM32 can switch off/restart the Pi branch. A shared battery or STM32 failure remains a common failure mode. |
| Camera/IMU alignment target ≤0.5 ms | Hardware camera trigger, IMU data-ready capture and GNSS PPS capture use STM32 timers. Linux arrival timestamps are insufficient. |
| Three-hour endurance | Approximately 24 Wh protected 2S Li-ion pack; efficient buck conversion. This supports a roughly 6 W average load after a conservative combined 20% energy/conversion derating. Runtime is not established until measured. |
| Scientific temperature profile | A small external NTC probe in ventilated, radiation-shielded air, thermally separated from the Pi. Qualify its installed response and calibration; barometer die temperature is only for pressure compensation. |
| Narrow cylindrical enclosure | Mount the 65 × 30 mm Pi vertically: its rectangular diagonal exceeds 66 mm. Use a welded battery pack, retained cables and mechanically secured optics. Fit must be checked in your mechanical model. |
| Passive descent | No motor, servo or deployment drivers are included. |

Power path: **protected 2S pack → master switch → fuse/reverse protection → independent 5.1 V and 3.3 V bucks**. The 5.1 V branch supplies Pi/camera through a controllable load switch. A 3.0 V LDO from the always-on 3.3 V rail supplies the thermistor ADC and buffer.

## Connections and operating choices that affect the BOM

1. **Timing:** STM32 timer output drives camera XTR through the 1.5 kΩ/1.8 kΩ divider. The official camera requires 1.8 V trigger logic; exposure duration is trigger-low duration plus 14.26 µs. About 485.74 µs low therefore requests 500 µs exposure. Drive trigger inactive/low as appropriate while camera power is absent to avoid back-powering. Apply the documented R11 change only if the camera revision has Q2 populated. Verify exposure timing/frame association with a scope and an optical timing target. [Official trigger documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html).
2. **Sensor buses:** dedicate an SPI bus to BMI088, with **separate accelerometer/gyroscope chip-selects and captured data-ready inputs**. Apply Bosch's data-synchronisation configuration or log the two sample streams with separate timestamps; they are not automatically simultaneous. Use separate chip-selects for top-board radio and middle-board flash on SPI2, and validate the short inter-board SPI connection. Put MS5611 on a 3.3 V I²C bus, ADS1115 on a separate bus pulled to its 3.0 V supply. Optional LIS3MDL shares the MS5611 bus. Capture PPS and maintain sequence-numbered, CRC-protected UART packets to Pi. The [STM32-to-Pi link protocol](stm32_pi_link_protocol.md) defines the current framing and recommends 230400 baud; consider 460800 only after a full-load bench test. This supersedes the guide's earlier 921600-baud example. The [connection guide](flight_computer_connections.md) records proposed RF contact mapping; verify the RF endpoint against its saved schematic.
3. **Thermistor:** 3.0 V pulsed excitation → precision 150 kΩ resistor → TE 10 kΩ probe → ground; buffer the divider node. Measure excitation and divider during each pulse. At 25°C the calculated probe dissipation is **3.52 µW** while on; worst-case over resistance is **15 µW**. Duty cycling to ≤6% reduces average self-heating. Allow actual analogue settling and ADC conversions before switching off; validate the temperature range, installed air response and calibration. This probe needs its own R(T) calibration, not FP07 coefficients. See the linked digital option before populating temperature-only electronics if changing implementation.
4. **Pressure port:** use a static-pressure vent protected from direct flow and light. At 10 m/s, air dynamic pressure is about 60 Pa, equivalent to roughly 5 m altitude near sea level—far larger than sensor noise. An airtight box or ram-facing hole defeats either barometer. Timestamp conversion timing and avoid aggressive filters that lag the descent. Confirm JLC's assembly profile meets MS5611's 250°C/40-second solder limit, keep washing/coating out of the port, and allow post-reflow recovery before pressure calibration.
5. **GNSS RF:** use the active-antenna feed circuit and placement from the [u-blox integration manual](https://content.u-blox.com/sites/default/files/MAX-M10S_IntegrationManual_UBX-20053088.pdf). Confirm antenna current is within VCC_RF capability, logic configuration is 3.3 V, and backup supply wiring follows the manual. No separate backup battery is needed. Maintain GNSS as a separately labelled truth data stream.
6. **Radio:** the earlier proposal used **869.525 MHz, LoRa SF7, BW125 kHz, CR4/5, explicit header, CRC, eight-symbol preamble** with a 32-byte payload at 1 Hz (71.936 ms, 7.1936% duty). Current `code/cansat/telemetry.py` sends a **40-byte downlink packet**. The 11 October power budget calculates **82.176 ms (8.2176% at 1 Hz)** for 40 bytes with SF7/BW125/CR4/5. Treat the old 32-byte figure as a historical sizing assumption; it is not the current radio payload. The 32-byte Nav payload in the [STM32-to-Pi link protocol](stm32_pi_link_protocol.md) is a different field set on a different link. Ofcom IR2030/1/19 (official PDF, checked 13 October 2026) permits airborne use in 869.40–869.65 MHz subject to conditions, with a 500 mW ERP maximum and a ≤10% duty-cycle alternative to equivalent spectrum-access/interference-mitigation techniques. The candidate 1 Hz/40-byte airtime is below 10% before retries, but channel allocation, total airtime, ERP, equipment conditions and event requirements still need verification; do not treat the estimate as evidence of compliance. Obtain the organiser's channel allocation and budget all transmissions/retries. [Ofcom IR2030, published 7 April 2026](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/spectrum/interface-requirements/ir-2030.pdf).
7. **Boot and power:** primary logging/telemetry starts on STM32 without waiting for Linux. Power the Pi at system switch-on after supply checks, finish Linux/service startup before loading, and leave it powered throughout pad waiting and launch. Idle image processing does not mean Pi power-off; default firmware does not power-cycle on UART silence. Disable UART buffers before removing Pi power. Request clean shutdown where possible and retain a read-only OS plus separate data partition. Do not connect a second USB 5 V source in parallel with your buck without power-source isolation. Log low battery and stop heavy processing before pack protection trips.
8. **Camera processing:** capture the full field then resize for matching rather than blindly cropping to 1280 × 800 and losing ground coverage. The proposed camera/lens changes the original 600 × 375 m footprint; rebuild intrinsics and undistortion maps. Bound image queues, store the map in tiles and measure worst-case first-fix time on the selected Pi.

## Mass, power and cost checks

**Power:** the later [11 October mass/power budget](mass_power_budget.md) is the current estimate: 23.1/19.6/14.8 Wh usable energy across low/typical/high cases, 4.3 Wh typical use for three hours with the Pi idle plus flight and a one-hour beacon, and 335 g expected mass. These are calculated/assumed, not weighed or metered; the current estimate supersedes the rough sizing here. The three-hour full-load case remains a separate risk.

**Mass:** roughly 100 g battery, 10–15 g Pi, 34 g camera, plus lens/adapter, both custom PCBs, inter-board connector/supports, antennas, wires and retention. These leave a plausible allocation for enclosure and parachute under 350 g, but the complete mechanical BOM is not yet weighed. Target about **330 g total** and add securely fixed ballast only if below 300 g. The original proposal's ~20 g camera addition no longer applies. Verify the whole camera/lens/pack stack inside 115 mm before manufacturing the PCB.

**Cost:** the mandatory IC/module rows sum to about **$45.49** across both boards: **MAIN $27.80 + RF $17.69** at the checked one-unit prices, before support parts, the inter-board connector and both PCB fabrication/assembly quotes. This is approximately **$12.64 less** than the previous IC selection because BMI088 replaces ICM-42688-P; the stock-footprint buzzer adds approximately $2.31 elsewhere in the support BOM. Prices mix the explicitly dated 1/3 October snapshots. Optics are significant: the referenced lens is $87.40 and adapter $32.75 before tax/shipping, with the lock nut extra. Do not claim compliance with £400 from IC prices alone: obtain an assembly quote including extended-part fees and a landed UK quote for the battery and optics, then include enclosure/parachute and all flight hardware. A cheaper lens is possible only after verifying image-circle coverage, focus and distortion; an arbitrary CCTV lens is not an equivalent substitution.

The remaining engineering gates are the **5 Hz Pi processing benchmark, three-hour full-system runtime, actual optical focus/fit/calibration, final mass/cost, and final schematic/layout review**. The dated component stock checks cover the selected PCB electronic parts; MAIN J4 is selected, while its compatible RF mating part/cable and fabrication/assembly costs remain unresolved or unquoted. These checks are not evidence that the engineering gates have already passed.
